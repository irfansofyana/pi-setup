# Natural writing

1. Identify the deliverable, audience and format. Apply this skill to prose being drafted or revised, not tool results, code-only tasks, exact-output requests, or every conversational reply.
2. Preserve source meaning: numbers, units, negation, uncertainty, attribution, citations and logical relationships. Keep qualifiers such as "may", "likely" and "unknown" when they carry evidence or confidence, even under compression; remove only empty hedging. Do not invent experiences, opinions, metrics, evidence or anecdotes. Retain the author's supplied viewpoint. Ask for missing facts only when necessary to produce the deliverable accurately.
3. Replace stock openings, inflated praise, vague filler and repetitive scaffolding with concrete wording grounded in supplied content. Prefer direct verbs when clearer; retain useful passive voice, adverbs and technical vocabulary. Patterns are editing clues, not grammar bans. Match the audience rather than forcing informality.
4. Avoid em dashes in generated prose; prefer commas, colons or periods. Preserve code, commands, identifiers, URLs, structured data, metadata, citations and verbatim quoted text byte-for-byte, including punctuation and flags.
5. Caveman owns compression. When active, preserve fragments, shorthand and length constraints; otherwise match the requested prose style. Preserve full clarity for safety warnings and consequential actions. Never toggle Caveman, read/write its preferences, or introduce style state.
6. Before returning, compare every material claim and protected literal against the source; correct changed certainty, unsupported additions and altered literals. Confirm requested format and active style. Return only the requested deliverable unless the user asks for an editing audit.

When modifying or evaluating this skill, read [provenance and evaluation guidance](references/sources.md).
